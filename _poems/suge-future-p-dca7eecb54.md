  Death does not knock.
  It waits.
  It waits in the hum of machines,
  in the pauses between a heartbeat.
  It waits in the way your name
  stays too long in someone’s throat
  and never comes out again.

  It is not a stranger.
  It wears your mother’s hands
  when she folds the blanket of someone
  who will no longer sleep.
  It is the smell of burnt coffee
  in a hospital hallway,
  the silence after a phone call
  you don’t want to answer.

  Death is not cruel.
  It is a mirror,
  and when it stands beside you,
  it will not speak.
  It will only ask,
  Did you live?
  Did you leave the world softer than you found it?
  Did you love so fiercely
  that your absence will tear the sky?


  It does not break you.
  It sculpts you.
  It strips you bare,
  until all that remains
  is the fire you forgot to tend,
  the light you never let out,
  the truth you spent years running from.

  You will see it,
  in the reflection of your child’s tears,
  in the tremble of a lover’s hands
  as they hold yours for the last time.
  You will feel it,
  in the weight of every unsaid word,
  every unfinished kiss,
  every thread of time
  you thought you had.

  But death is not an end.
  It is a hand,
  steady and strong,
  that sets you down gently
  into the arms of those who remain.

  And when it leaves,
  it will carry only your silence,
  your shadows,
  your truths.

  So when death stands beside you,
  do not turn away.
  Meet its gaze,
  and let it remind you:
  You are still alive.
  You are the fire.
  Now burn.

  3 February 2025
