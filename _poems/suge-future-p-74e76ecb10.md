verse: |-
  Every night I enter
  the land where I am
  both born and dying,
  where time is neither time
  nor timeless,
  but the pause
  between heartbeats.

  Here, the dead teach calculus
  to unborn children,
  and my grandmother
  peels apples with tomorrow's knife,
  each spiral of skin
  rising into yesterday's light.

  Time is a broken compass
  spinning backward
  toward the first word
  God forgot to speak—
  that syllable which holds
  all weather,
  all seasons,
  the memory of snow
  before winter learned to fall.

  I become
  the echo of my mother's pulse
  before her heart knew my name,
  before name knew
  the weight of being,
  before being learned
  to forget itself.

  When dawn arrives
  with its cargo of erasure,
  its liturgy of light,
  I surface holding
  only the shape of loss—

  that perfect sphere
  where all our unlived lives
  press against the membrane
  of what we dare
  to call real,
  what we dare
  to call home,
  what we dare
  to call
  ourselves.

  27 JUL 2025
